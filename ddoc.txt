BLACKWING
The tale of Eden’s unsung orbital vanguards 
Title: Blackwing (working title), Overwatch, Shadow Wings, High Guard, Seers of Eden  Tagline: The tale of Eden’s unsung orbital vanguards  Abstract: Blackwing’s premise is that of the unusual post-orbital, pre-FTL position of the theorized Space Guard, national or international space forces designed to keep the home planet safe from natural or directed asteroid strikes, spacecraft-enabled terrorist attacks, advanced technology gunrunners, and other criminal or illegal activity capable of high casualties up to extinction-level events. Players assume the role of an officer in the space corps of one of Eden’s four major spacefaring empires and try to juggle their Reputation in the admiralty and Fame among the masses, affected by their successes and failures on-mission, the Ammunition and Fuel of their ship, Morale and Experience of their overworked crew, and the plethora of threats to Eden that lurk among the stars – whether they come from NPC terrorists, industrial accidents, or other players at the behest of their governments. Squadrons, essentially alliances, can unite under a common banner to effect Reputation and Fame bonuses and same-spot deployment for community purposes.
Resources: Blackwing centers around two character resources, Reputation and Fame, two ship elements, Ammunition and Fuel, and two crew resources, Morale and Experience. Each of these has a different role to play in terms of player-crew interaction, player-world interaction, and player-government interaction.  -Reputation represents notability within the officer corps of the player’s parent nation (as an integer), and raises with successful interceptions of asteroids, ship bombers, enemy government craft, and accident and rescue aid. It lowers with failed missions, operating continually outside the patrol zone, diplomatic blunders, and failure to help allied vessels. If Reputation hits zero, the officer is an official disgrace and is drummed out of the Space Guard (player defeated). It can be spent like currency to call in for ship upgrades, favors like fire support or useful goods, and replacement ships if a mission goes sour. Reputation does not decay like Fame.  -Fame represents notability to the general public of the parent nation, and raises with high-profile successes like single-handedly stopping an asteroid, taking on an enemy contingent alone, “last-minute saves” like intercepting a ship bomber one square away from Eden, and scoring victories outside the patrol zone. Fame is also raised during high-profile failures, though not as much. Fame lowers when time passes without more Fame having been accrued. It acts as a multiplier – a famous captain, for good or bad, will be given even more Reputation than normal for an average success, and will be subtracted even more Reputation than normal for an average failure. Fame pressures players not to slip up, lest their notoriety causes the admiralty to deal harshly with them while in the public eye.  -Ammunition is the amount of missiles, slugs, shells, laser capacitors, et cetera, that your ship has onboard, expressed as a percentage. Each attack on a stellar object, ship, or station will subtract a fixed amount of Ammunition, depending on the type of target. When Ammunition falls below 15%, the attacks from the player ship suffer a debuff and the player will be notified. When Ammunition hits 0%, attacks will not be possible and players must flee or be destroyed during enemy encounters. Ammunition can be replenished without cost at any of the player’s government’s space stations or resupply ships, or lent to the player at a cost of Reputation from allied ships.  -Fuel is the amount of reaction mass onboard your ship, allowing it to move through Eden’s star system from sector to sector, expressed as a percentage. A small amount of fuel is also subtracted when moving to each in-sector mission. Each sector movement subtracts a fixed amount of fuel, double for sectors adjacent to planets or stars. When Fuel falls below 10%, only half-speed movement will be possible and the player will be notified. Fuel can be replenished without cost at any of the player’s government’s space stations or resupply ships, or lent to the player at a cost of Reputation from allied ships.  -Morale represents your crew’s belief in their ability and your leadership as a percentage. Morale above 50% positively impacts ship attack and defense, while Morale below 50% negatively impacts ship attack and defense. Morale is boosted by successes and lowered by failures, but is always positively bolstered by the amount of Fame the player has. Whether good or bad, a more famous captain raises the crew’s Morale. If a player’s ship is lost, the new ship will have a new crew and thus a reset Morale level.  -Experience represents your crew’s familiarity and veteran skills with their ship and their duties (as an integer). Experience increases with both successes and failures, though successes gain much more Experience than failures. Experience positively impacts ship attack, defense, and speed. Experience cannot be lost unless the player’s ship is lost, in which case their new ship will have a new crew and thus an Experience of zero.
  Gameplay: A new account will start off with introduction, background, and tutorials, then place the player in control of a patrol corvette of the nation of their choosing with a green crew, full tanks of fuel and ammo, and a handful of Reputation in a starting spacedock orbiting Eden. They will be assigned a wide patrol sector in Eden’s star system where they are expected to pursue terrorists, smugglers, and gunrunners at their own discretion, respond to civil and military emergencies, and deal with high-level emergencies when called upon by Command.
Players can move to their patrol sector without fuel or time cost. There they will find ship traffic, most civil, some insurgent or criminal in disguise, space stations both official and unsavory, mining, processing, and industrial production facilities that could suffer an accident at a moment’s notice, and any other players in the same patrol sector. How they deal with incidents that arise, and those they create themselves, in large part determine their Reputation among the admiralty and their Fame among the people.
-Some crises will be generated randomly – a distress signal leads players to a freighter being attacked by pirates, a nanoconstructor leak threatens an entire orbital factory complex and hundreds of workers, a flight of “rogue” enemy government ships threatens to overrun the sector, high-profile terrorists plan to sneak a thermonuclear-equipped freighter through to Eden low orbit. These will be presented to the player as missions available in the sector upon login. Though the sector is large, far less Fuel is used moving between missions than between sectors. 
If a player accepts a given generated mission, they will be given exposition and a small briefing before being forced to make a decision, usually between several options. If a freighter is being attacked by pirates, for example, the player could attack directly and hope they outgun them, attempt to deceive the pirates into fleeing by posing as part of a larger formation (the success of which is determined by crew Experience and luck), attempt to reason with/threaten the pirates (positively affected by Fame), or let them plunder the cargo and save the crew.
Post mission, Command comments on the outcome of the incident and awards or subtracts Reputation, largely depending on the outcome and amplified by Fame. Between missions, players can spend Reputation to refuel or rearm on station, apply for shore leave (ability to move to any sector without Reputation penalty from Command for x days, more likely to be granted as more time is spent on-station), apply for resupply time (ability to move back to Eden core sectors without Reputation penalty from Command for x days, more likely to be granted when Ammunition and/or Fuel drops below critical levels), acquire better ship components or even new ships altogether.
-Some missions will be assigned as open missions from Command to any ships within a certain range of the event – such as massive pirate or foreign government attacks, asteroids naturally or artificially inclined to strike Eden, etc. These will have Eden’s media focused on them, so Reputation and Fame rewards are both greatly enhanced for whoever ends the crisis.
-The player can also undertake their own missions by travelling to any of these ships, stations, or other sites themselves and investigating. A player may, for instance, check up on a civil shipping lane and notice a freighter with a high radiation signature. The ship’s captain might inform the player that it is due to old reactor shielding, and the player will have to decide whether to board and search the ship, leave it alone, or flag it as suspect to their nation’s Port Authority. The player’s success or failure centers around whether the situation is harmless or dangerous beneath the surface. 
If the player boards the vessel and it has nothing illegal onboard, they will suffer a massive Reputation loss from Command and huge embarrassment. If they board the vessel and find a terrorist nuke, however, they will be lauded as heroes, with both Reputation and Fame going up. If they leave it alone and it is harmless, they might get a Reputation bonus from the grateful captain spreading positive word about the player. If they leave it alone and it carries a terrorist nuke, however, they will suffer a huge Reputation loss when it reaches Eden and is either caught at the Port Authority or detonates in midmorning traffic. It is worth noting that players will not be penalized for threats that get through their sector that they do not investigate – only if they move to that object and then choose the let through option.
-If relations between two governments deteriorate into open war (either as a plot event or a series of political blunders by players), Command may assign missions to attack NPC enemy ships or facilities in a more frontline military role.
-Squadrons are much like alliances, and are given significant leeway in pursuing their objectives. Squadrons can name themselves, choose a patrol sector or sectors for all of their members to be stationed in, build stations, facilities, and NPC ships in those sectors at a cost of Reputation, and engage in combat with player squadrons of the same nation (live-fire “war games”) or player squadrons of other nations (privateering).
-Player ships stay in-game when a player logs off, and can be attacked while logged off, but can only be attacked x times in total between logins to avoid ganking.
