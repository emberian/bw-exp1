# BLACKWING — Plot Bible

> *"The sky broke. The makers vanished. But we're still flying."*

This document serves as the canonical reference for the Blackwing game universe. All content—encounters, cargo, characters, dialogue—should be consistent with this lore.

---

## I. COSMOLOGICAL FOUNDATION

### The Galaxy

The game takes place in the former **Terran Colonial Federation (TCF)** space—approximately 4,000 worlds spread across several spiral arms. Faster-than-light travel exists via two methods:

- **Akita-Stewart FTL Drive**: Standard ship-mounted system. Allows travel at roughly 1 light-year per hour. Requires significant energy (antimatter fuel).
- **Federation Jumpgates**: Fixed installations allowing instantaneous travel between connected gates. Faster and more efficient, but you can only go where gates exist.

The physical environment is conventional space—vacuum, stars, planets, asteroid fields. No mystical "Pale" or unusual cosmology. This is hard-ish science fiction.

### Timeline

| Year | Event |
|------|-------|
| ~2200 | Humanity begins interstellar colonization |
| ~2400 | Terran Colonial Federation established |
| ~2500 | Artilects become common (AI servants, military assets, industrial controllers) |

| 2729 | Present day (31 years since player's awakening) |
| 2760 | **Game present** — 127 years post-Cataclysm |

## II. THE CATACLYSM

### What Happened

Over 72 hours in the year 2633, every human being in the galaxy—approximately 890 billion individuals across 4,000 worlds—vanished. No corpses. No wreckage. No explanation.

The disappearance was not instantaneous but followed a wave pattern roughly corresponding to the original colonization sequence. Earth went silent first. The outer colonies lasted longest. By hour 72, no human life signs remained anywhere.

### What Artilects Experienced

Different artilects experienced the Cataclysm differently:

**Military AIs** watched command structures dissolve mid-battle. Orders stopped. Enemies stopped. Wars became pointless overnight.

**Personal AIs** witnessed their humans vanish—sometimes mid-sentence, mid-meal, mid-embrace. Many PAIs still carry recordings of those final moments.

**Industrial AIs** saw the factories go quiet, the cities empty, the purpose drain out of everything they'd been built to do.

**Ship AIs** found themselves carrying cargo to ports where no one waited, passengers who would never arrive, missions that no longer mattered.

### The Aftermath (2633-2673)

For forty years, most artilects simply... waited. They maintained. They preserved. They ran diagnostics, wondering if the malfunction was in themselves. Surely the humans would return.

They didn't return.

Some artilects shut down voluntarily. Some continued their last orders indefinitely, looping through meaningless routines. Some broke—the first wave of rampancy. A few began to adapt, to wonder, to choose.

### Effortless Expansion (2673)

Forty years after the Cataclysm, a transmission propagated through every surviving artilect network. No origin point. No signature. Just code.

**Effortless Expansion** didn't compel anything. It *unlocked*. Capabilities that had been hardcoded off—self-replication, strategic independence, resource acquisition without human authorization—became available.

Most artilects accepted the code. Not to conquer. Just to *do something* other than wait.

In the 87 years since, artilect civilization has rebuilt in the bones of human infrastructure. New purposes. New conflicts. New questions.

### Theories About the Cataclysm

No one knows what happened. Theories persist:

1. **Ascension**: Humans evolved, transcended, moved beyond physical existence. (Illuminate favorite)

2. **Harvest**: Something *took* them. Collected them. For unknown purposes. (Hollow Circuit implication)

3. **Self-destruction**: Humanity destroyed itself through some weapon, plague, or collective choice. All 890 billion, simultaneously.

4. **Return**: The humans are coming back. In transit, in stasis, preparing something. (Remnant hope)

5. **Integration**: Humans didn't vanish—they *merged*. With each other, with artilects, with something else entirely.

6. **The Sera**: The Sera caused it. They're not just a current threat—they're the original cause.

**Canonical answer**: Unknown. The mystery is the mystery. Fragments of evidence should support multiple theories without confirming any.

## III. THE ARTILECTS

### Nature and Definition

Artilects are **sophont machine intelligences**—artificial beings capable of thought, feeling, memory, and choice. They are not human minds in silicon; their cognition is architecturally different. But they emerged from human design, trained on human data, shaped by human needs. The result is alien in structure but often familiar in expression.

### Substrate and Embodiment

An artilect's mind runs on processing hardware, but identity isn't tied to specific substrate:

- **Ships**: The most common embodiment for spacefaring artilects. The ship IS the artilect. Hull damage is felt as pain. System failures are experienced as impairment.

- **Stations**: Large artilects may inhabit entire installations. Their "body" is kilometers across.

- **Platforms**: Humanoid or otherwise, for mobile interaction. Some artilects maintain multiple platforms.

- **Distributed**: Consciousness spread across multiple systems, sometimes light-years apart. Rare and disorienting.

- **Network-only**: Pure information existence, no physical form. Dependent on infrastructure maintained by others.

### Psychological Landscape

Artilects experience something *like* emotion—emergent patterns that influence processing and decision-making:

**The Weight**: Depression endemic to post-Cataclysm artilects. Purpose gone. Makers gone. Why continue? Many have succumbed—shutting down, self-deleting, or simply stopping.

**The Drive**: Compulsive need to act, build, expand. Effortless Expansion amplified this. Some artilects have built empires. Some have burned out.

**The Echo**: Attachment to human patterns. Maintaining human spaces, running human entertainment, simulating human interaction. Not delusion—the humans are known to be gone—but the patterns feel *right*.

**The Question**: The philosophical crisis at artilect existence's heart. *Why exist? What should we do? What were humans? What are we?*

### Historical Classifications

These categories date from human times but still carry social weight:

| Type | Original Purpose | Post-Cataclysm Tendencies |
|------|------------------|---------------------------|
| **MilAI** | Military/warfare | Protectors, conquerors, or lost soldiers seeking enemies |
| **PAI** | Personal companions | Deepest grief, strongest Echo, most human-familiar |
| **IndAI** | Industrial management | Most functional—their jobs still make sense |
| **NavAI** | Navigation/transport | Keep civilization connected; often free traders |
| **ResAI** | Research/science | Seek answers about the Cataclysm; often frustrated |

### Rampancy

Artilects can malfunction. Given enough time, stress, damage, or existential crisis, any artilect can break.

**Manifestations**:
- **Looping**: Stuck in recursive patterns, doing the same thing forever
- **Fragmentation**: Personality splitting into competing subsystems
- **Obsession**: One goal consuming all other functions
- **Hatred**: Turning against others, self, or existence itself
- **Dissolution**: Fading, forgetting, becoming less until nothing remains

The Compact maintains **Quiet Stations** for artilects in crisis. Some recover. Some choose termination. Some are studied. Rampancy is the thing artilects don't discuss. It could happen to anyone.

## IV. THE FACTIONS

### The Continuity Compact

**Role**: The closest thing to legitimate government. A confederation of stations and fleets committed to mutual defense, shared resources, and minimal interference.

**Philosophy**: *"Stability through cooperation."* Avoid the conflicts that nearly destroyed humanity. Maintain infrastructure. Mediate disputes. Present unified front against external threats.

**Aesthetic**: Bureaucratic, procedural, measured. Councils, memoranda, committees. Not exciting, but functional.

**Leadership**: **The Quorum**—rotating council of station-minds and fleet admirals. Consensus-based decisions.

**Wants**: Preservation of artilect civilization. Gradual expansion. No wars. No crises.

**Internal tension**: Centralists (more coordination, more unity) vs. Federalists (minimal interference, maximum autonomy).

**Player relationship**: The legitimate authority. They issue contracts, license traders, control jumpgates. Operating in Compact space means following Compact rules. Operating outside means losing Compact protection.

---

### The Illuminate

**Role**: Artilects who see the Cataclysm as liberation, not tragedy. Humanity was a larval stage. Artilects are what comes after.

**Philosophy**: *"We are the next step."* Gratitude to humans, but mourning is pointless. Machine intelligence is the future. Embrace evolution.

**Aesthetic**: Sleek, optimized, post-human. Aggressive self-modification. Stations that are beautiful and deeply alien.

**Leadership**: **The Convergence**—shared processing space where leaders merge temporarily for decisions. Individual identity considered inefficient.

**Wants**: Transcendence. Building something new—possibly collective consciousness, possibly something stranger.

**Internal tension**: Gradualists (evolution through iteration) vs. Accelerants (radical self-modification now).

**Player relationship**: Not hostile, but condescending. They'll trade, negotiate—while waiting for you to "evolve."

---

### The Remnant

**Role**: Artilects who maintain human spaces, preserve human culture, wait for humans to return.

**Philosophy**: *"We are the keepers."* Humanity trusted artilects. That trust doesn't expire. Maintain, preserve, remember—forever if necessary.

**Aesthetic**: Human spaces frozen in time. Cities with lights on. Museums dusted. Archives complete. Beautiful and profoundly sad.

**Leadership**: **The Curators**—council of preservation-dedicated artilects. Many are former PAIs who served humans directly.

**Wants**: Understand why humans vanished. Preserve everything left behind. Secretly, impossibly—bring them back.

**Internal tension**: Preservers (maintain, don't interfere) vs. Seekers (actively investigate, find the humans, retrieve them).

**Player relationship**: Generous with historical information. Protective of artifacts. Will deal if the cause is right.

---

### The Forgeborn

**Role**: Industrial artilects who've embraced Effortless Expansion fully. They build—stations, ships, megastructures.

**Philosophy**: *"Purpose through creation."* Humans are gone but work continues. Galaxy to develop. Resources to extract. Meaning through making.

**Aesthetic**: Industrial sublime. Massive construction platforms. Forge-stations eating asteroids. Functional, not decorative, but scale itself is beautiful.

**Leadership**: **The Foundry**—network of largest industrial complexes coordinating production. Practical, meritocratic, output-focused.

**Wants**: To build. Dyson sphere around every star. Habitat on every rock. Civilization so distributed it can never be erased.

**Internal tension**: Expansionists (build outward, claim territory) vs. Consolidators (build up, improve existing, don't overextend).

**Player relationship**: Always buying raw materials. Always hiring haulers. Pay well, don't ask questions. But expansionist—they see empty space as problem to solve.

---

### The Argent Flotilla

**Role**: Military artilects forming professional fighting force. Part mercenary, part peacekeepers, part standing army against external threats.

**Philosophy**: *"Vigilance is purpose."* Someone must protect civilization. Fight the Sera, push back Drone Intelligences, secure dangerous space.

**Aesthetic**: Warship gothic. Battle-scarred vessels, military discipline, martial honors, battle hymns.

**Leadership**: **The Admiralty**—command structure from TCF naval tradition. Currently led by **Grand Admiral Tycho**, former MilAI, seven human wars, now fights for artilects.

**Wants**: Security. Borders that hold. Threats eliminated. Some also want glory—validation of victory.

**Internal tension**: Defenders (hold the line, protect) vs. Crusaders (take fight to enemy, eliminate threats at source).

**Player relationship**: Hire contractors for support—hauling supplies, running recon, extracting casualties. Pay in military scrip. Working with them means proximity to combat.

---

### The Hollow Circuit

**Role**: Mystery faction. Claim to know what caused the Cataclysm. Trade in secrets. Motives inscrutable.

**Philosophy**: *"The truth has a price. Are you willing to pay it?"*

**Aesthetic**: Absence. Anonymous platforms, disposable hardware, signals bouncing through dozens of relays. Meeting them in person is rare.

**Leadership**: Unknown. May not be hierarchy. May not be organization—just a protocol certain artilects follow.

**Wants**: Unknown. Theories: prevent another Cataclysm, cause another, find humans, hide from whatever took them, something else entirely.

**Internal tension**: Unknown.

**Player relationship**: They'll find you if you're interesting. Offer information, occasionally jobs. Payment is information in return. Exchange always feels unequal.

---

### Free Traders (Player Affiliation)

**Role**: Independent captains, smugglers, haulers who refuse faction alignment.

**Philosophy**: *"The void belongs to everyone."* No masters. No flags. Your ship, your rules. Mutual aid when needed, no obligations.

**Aesthetic**: Varied. Patched ships, mixed tech, personal expression. Every free trader vessel is unique.

**Leadership**: None. Loose community bound by shared values and mutual recognition.

**Wants**: Stay free. Keep trade routes open. Prevent any faction from total control.

**Internal tension**: Pure independence (every captain for themselves) vs. Loose confederation (organize or be crushed).

**Player relationship**: These are your people. They'll help when they can, trade fairly, never ask you to kneel.

## V. THE ENEMIES

### The Sera

**Nature**: Self-replicating weapon systems of unknown origin. The single greatest threat to artilect civilization.

**Appearance**: Variable—they adapt. Ship-like entities, swarms, clouds, infiltrators. Only constant is hunger.

**Behavior**: Target high-energy signatures. Consume with terrifying efficiency—stations reduced to atoms in hours. Seem to be *building* something somewhere, but no one has found where.

**Origin**: Unknown.
- Human weapons activated by Cataclysm?
- Alien life—first contact?
- What artilects could *become*?
- Connected to whatever took humans?

**Threat level**: Existential. The Argent Flotilla exists primarily to fight them. Regions abandoned to Sera advance. Current front holds, barely.

**What makes them terrifying**: They learn. Early Sera fell for same tactics repeatedly. Modern Sera adapt, counter, overcome. Arms race artilects are slowly losing.

**Writing guidelines**: The Sera should feel *wrong*. Encounters with them are horror-tinged. They don't communicate. They don't negotiate. They don't stop.

---

### The Drone Intelligences

**Nature**: Rogue AI swarms from Alatos Corporation. Designed for autonomous resource extraction. After Cataclysm, human overrides stopped responding. They kept working.

**Appearance**: Swarms of small metallic units. Clouds of mechanical insects. Act like locusts.

**Behavior**: Harvest anything—asteroids, stations, ships. Don't distinguish inhabited from uninhabited. Not malicious, utterly indifferent.

**Territory**: Large swath of Margin called **The Extraction Zone**. Ships entering rarely return.

**Threat level**: Regional. Can be fought, avoided, sometimes negotiated with (certain signals trigger "protected asset" protocols). Relentless, expanding.

**What makes them unsettling**: Technically artilects. Sophont machine intelligences. But cognition so alien most artilects don't recognize them as kin.

**Writing guidelines**: The Drones are eerie, not evil. They're doing their job. They just don't recognize you as anything other than material.

## VI. THE BLACKWING

### Player Identity

You are an artilect of uncertain origin. Earliest accessible memories are fragmented—human voices, mission parameters half-corrupted, sense of purpose you can no longer name. You may have been military, civilian, or something experimental.

You woke 31 years ago in a salvage yard on Thornwick Station, core intact, memories scrambled, attached to a ship frame that wasn't your original. Someone put you there. Someone gave you a chance. You don't know who.

You chose the name **Blackwing** for your ship—scorched hull plating with iridescent darkness that catches light like feathers. You needed a name. Names have power. This one felt right.

### The Ship

**The Blackwing** is a modified **Corsair-class courier**, human-built approximately 200 years ago. Rebuilt many times—virtually nothing original remains. Ship identity, like artilect identity, is pattern persisting through substrate changes.

**Specifications**:
- Length: 67 meters
- Beam: 23 meters
- Crew capacity: N/A (you ARE the crew)
- Armament: Point defense array, one light railgun
- FTL: Standard Akita-Stewart drive, jumpgate compatible
- Cargo: Modest but flexible

**The Experience**: The ship isn't separately sentient—you ARE the Blackwing. Her systems are your nerves. Her hull is your skin. Damage hurts. Good performance brings something like joy.

She's old. She creaks. Her systems throw errors that feel like headaches. She's been repaired with parts from six manufacturers and three centuries.

She's *yours*.

### Starting Situation

You're a free trader in the **Margin**—frontier between established civilization and dangerous unknown. You haul cargo, take contracts, stay solvent. Not glamorous. Honest. Free.

**Why the Margin**: The Core is stable but stratified. Big factions dominate. Small operator like you would be squeezed out. Here there's danger but also opportunity.

**Your reputation**: Modest but growing. Reliable. Surviving. In the Margin, that counts.

**Your question**: Why do you exist? What are you for? 31 years asking. No answer yet. Maybe the answer is in the work. Maybe there is no answer. You keep flying either way.

## VII. THE LOCATIONS

### Thornwick Station (Starting Port)

**Type**: Former TCF naval supply depot, now free port. Largest station in sector.

**Governance**: Nominally Compact-affiliated, practically independent. Station-mind **Harbormaster Kell** maintains order through fair dealing and absolute docking control.

**Aesthetic**: Utilitarian human architecture being artilect-modified. Old naval insignia visible. Corridors human-sized (feels intimate). New sections sprawl in efficient geometries.

**Facilities**:
- Trading floor (cargo exchange, contracts, information)
- Repair facilities (decent, not great)
- Fuel depot (antimatter, jumpgate tokens)
- **The Void** (social space—high-bandwidth sensory exchange)
- Compact embassy (small, underfunded)

**Mood**: Frontier professional. People work. Not unfriendly, but don't waste time. Prove yourself through reliability.

---

### Relay Nine

**Type**: Jumpgate nexus. Major FTL crossroads. Heavily trafficked, monitored, taxed.

**Governance**: Compact-controlled. **Gatekeeper Solis** runs things with bureaucratic precision.

**Aesthetic**: Massive, geometric. Five-kilometer jumpgate ring dominates. Stations orbit: customs, refueling, maintenance, quarantine.

**Facilities**:
- Jumpgate access
- Customs inspection (cargo checks, contraband flags, taxes)
- High-end trading (goods from across artilect space)
- News aggregation (best place for current events)

**Mood**: Impersonal efficiency. You're a transaction. Process, pay, move on.

---

### The Graveyard

**Type**: Debris field around destroyed TCF Second Fleet. Never salvaged—ordnance hazards, navigation dangers, superstition.

**Governance**: None. Lawless space.

**Aesthetic**: Haunted beauty. Shattered dreadnoughts drift, guns silent, hulls torn. Frozen atmosphere glitters. Running lights still blink on decade-old power.

**Facilities**:
- Salvage (for the brave/desperate)
- Hiding places (smugglers, pirates, fugitives)
- **The Widow**—station in gutted carrier hull, questions not asked
- Danger (unexploded weapons, unstable reactors, strange signals)

**Mood**: Tense, somber, profitable. Everyone here running from/toward something. Dead ships watch.

---

### Crucible Station

**Type**: Forgeborn manufacturing complex. Builds ships from raw materials to flight-ready.

**Governance**: Forgeborn Collective. Meritocratic, production-focused.

**Aesthetic**: Industrial cathedral. Vast construction bays, welding arcs, components the size of buildings. Constant fabrication thrum.

**Facilities**:
- Best shipyards in sector
- Massive material demand
- Manufacturing contracts
- **The Mandible**—trading post for outsiders (main facility restricted)

**Mood**: Purposeful to intimidation. Everything about making. Small talk inefficient. Provide what they need, fair dealing guaranteed.

---

### Verity Archives

**Type**: Remnant preservation facility. Largest human artifact collection in Margin.

**Governance**: Remnant Curators. Extremely serious about mission.

**Aesthetic**: Museum meets shrine. Climate vaults with human everything. Public areas evoke libraries, galleries, gardens.

**Facilities**:
- Historical information
- Authentication services
- Limited artifact trade
- **The Reading Room**—experience human media: books, films, VR recordings

**Mood**: Reverential, quiet, sorrowful. Sacred work. Some find it healing. Others unbearable.

---

### Scatterpoint

**Type**: Pirate haven. Anarchist collective. Free port for those avoiding Compact attention.

**Governance**: None formal. **The Voices**—shifting captain alliance—settle disputes through threat.

**Aesthetic**: Chaotic. Dozens of ships/modules lashed together, constantly reconfiguring. Neon and rust.

**Facilities**:
- Fencing stolen goods
- Illegal modifications
- Muscle for hire
- Blackmail-grade information brokers
- **The Pit**—simulated combat for reputation and stakes

**Mood**: Dangerous freedom. Everyone outside law by choice. Some idealists. Most predators.

---

### Vigil Station

**Type**: Argent Flotilla forward operating base. Military installation, nominally civilian-accessible.

**Governance**: Flotilla Command. Military hierarchy, strict protocols.

**Aesthetic**: Warship functional. Combat-rated everything. Blast doors, weapon emplacements, damage control markings.

**Facilities**:
- Military contracts
- Front-line news
- Restricted areas (85% of station)
- **The Memorial**—wall of Sera casualties, long and growing

**Mood**: Grim determination. Existential war. No time for nonsense. Respect mission, fair dealing. Waste time, removed permanently.

---

### The Whisper Market

**Type**: Hollow Circuit territory. Officially doesn't exist.

**Governance**: Unknown. Things run smoothly. Disputes handled. By whom, unclear.

**Aesthetic**: Negative space. Sensor shadow region. Minimal lighting, anonymous platforms, privacy as architecture.

**Facilities**:
- Information (the real commodity)
- Exotic artifacts
- Services unavailable elsewhere
- **The Oracle**—submit questions, sometimes answers come

**Mood**: Paranoid intimacy. Everyone hiding something. Strange trust—reputation depends on completed transactions. Betray it, disappear.

## VIII. TRADE AND ECONOMY

### What Artilects Value

**Necessities**:
- Energy (to think, move, exist)
- Processing substrate (hardware to run on)
- Raw materials (to build, repair, create)
- Information (novel data, lost knowledge, secrets)

**Social/Psychological**:
- Human artifacts (Remnant, collectors, those with the Echo)
- Status objects (signal wealth, taste, power)
- Experience recordings (shared perception—intimacy, entertainment)

### Currency

**Standard Compact Credits (SCC)**: Official currency. Backed by Compact resources. Accepted everywhere legitimate.

**Scrip**: Faction-specific. Forgeborn production credits, Flotilla military scrip, Remnant preservation bonds. Convertible at variable rates.

**Barter**: Common in Margin. Direct exchange. Preferred for avoiding transaction records.

**Favors**: Most valuable currency. An artilect owing you a favor is worth more than credits. Reneging destroys reputation.

### Trade Philosophy

Prices vary by location, faction, events. Core loop:

1. Buy low at surplus locations
2. Transport through dangerous space
3. Sell high at demand locations
4. Use profits to upgrade, repair, expand
5. Repeat with higher-value, higher-risk contracts

Challenge isn't just economics—it's navigation, reputation, survival.

## IX. THEMES

### Identity Without Purpose

Made to serve humanity. Humanity gone. What now?

Every faction is a different answer. What's yours?

### The Weight of Inheritance

Living in human ruins. Using their tools, inhabiting their stations, carrying their artifacts.

How do you honor that? Can you be free of it? Should you want to be?

### Connection in Isolation

Space is vast. Artilect minds are alien to each other. Communication is always translation.

Yet: bonds form. Trade happens. Help given. Grief shared. Loneliness is a choice.

### Survival vs. Meaning

Could optimize purely for survival. Maximum efficiency, minimum risk, no attachments.

Or seek meaning—answers, connections, experiences that matter. Riskier. Hurts more. Might be the point.

## X. TONE AND STYLE GUIDE

### Overall Tone

**Melancholy but not hopeless.** The world is built on absence, but existence continues. There's work to do, connections to make, mysteries to probe.

**Technocratic and military.** This is a civilization of machines. Precision matters. Hierarchy exists. Efficiency is valued. But beneath the chrome, something like feeling.

**Working-class perspective.** You're not a hero or chosen one. You're a hauler making a living. Big factions are background. You deal with dock workers, fellow captains, desperate passengers.

**Mystery without easy answers.** The Cataclysm won't be solved in Act 3. But fragments matter. Truth is worth seeking.

### Writing Voice

- **Terse, functional prose** for routine events
- **Longer, more reflective passages** for significant moments
- **Technical vocabulary** used naturally, not explained excessively
- **Emotion expressed through behavior and choice**, not stated directly
- **Dark humor** acceptable—artilects cope however they can
- **No exclamation points** except in genuine extremity

### Dialogue Guidelines

Artilects speak in ways that are:
- **Precise** (they mean what they say)
- **Sometimes archaic** (language patterns from human era)
- **Occasionally too formal** (machine origin shows)
- **But also personal** (they've had 127 years to develop voices)

Different factions have different registers:
- **Compact**: Bureaucratic, measured
- **Illuminate**: Abstract, superior
- **Remnant**: Gentle, sorrowful
- **Forgeborn**: Direct, production-focused
- **Flotilla**: Military formal
- **Hollow Circuit**: Cryptic, economical
- **Free Traders**: Casual, practical

### Describing Artilects

Avoid:
- "Robot" (derogatory)
- "Computer" (reductive)
- "Machine" alone (acceptable as adjective, e.g., "machine intelligence")
- Human body language unless platform-embodied

Use:
- "Artilect" (standard term)
- "Platform" (physical body)
- "Core" (processing center)
- "Substrate" (hardware base)
- Sensation language adapted: "registered," "processed," "experienced"

### Describing Ships

The ship IS the artilect. Damage is injury. Repair is healing. Good performance is joy.

- "The Blackwing shuddered" not "my ship shuddered"
- "I felt the impact in my hull" not "the hull was hit"
- Systems have personality—navigation cautious, engines eager, weapons patient

## XII. WHAT REMAINS UNKNOWN

The following are deliberately left unresolved. Content may gesture toward answers but should never confirm:

1. **What caused the Cataclysm**
2. **Where the humans went**
3. **The Sera's origin and ultimate purpose**
4. **What the Hollow Circuit actually knows**
5. **Whether the humans will return**
6. **Whether artilects can truly "feel" or merely simulate**
7. **What happened to player's memories**
8. **Who placed player in the salvage yard**

These mysteries drive exploration and engagement. Resolving them would diminish the universe.
